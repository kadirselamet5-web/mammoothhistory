About this map

MAMMOTH HISTORY LAB

Read discovery sites alongside their records.

This atlas presents all 84 data rows in Sheet1 of SOFT_TISSUE_Project_Database_2026_10_28_AO.xlsx, supplied as the Lab’s current soft-tissue dataset (workbook dated 2026-10-28; map built 2026-10-28). It shows documented find sites and discovery years alongside the specimen ages reported in the sources, and labels how each find position was obtained. It is not a comprehensive inventory of preserved megafauna.

Current dataset and record identity

The workbook contains 84 rows and 84 distinct Specimen_id values; 83 rows carry usable coordinates and 1 do not. The ID numbers are temporary checks for the Lab’s work in progress, not permanent specimen identifiers or citation keys. They remain visible for now and are not intended for the final public interface. The 6 taxon groups follow the workbook’s Species_type column, including Cave Lion/Felid, which also contains Homotherium latidens. The workbook’s Specimen_type_list sheet also lists Caribou and Elk, for which no soft-tissue records have been entered yet.

Compared with the previous edition (SOFT TISSUE Project_Database_2026_08_24 AO.xlsx): 6 IDs new to the map (20, 148, 149, 150, 151, 152); 1 earlier ID absent (131); 5 records with changed coordinates (79, 80, 81, 84, 119). No equivalence, renumbering or merge has been inferred between editions; the workbook values are used as supplied.

Coordinate provenance

Each row’s Location Provenance cell records how the find coordinate was obtained. The labels are reproduced as written:

  • source-stated: 22 records
  • historical map: 5 records
  • inferred/historical map: 6 records
  • inferred: 11 records
  • regional_placeholder: 39 records
  • unlocated: 1 record

The map draws source-stated coordinates as solid points, inferred and historical-map positions with a dashed ring, and regional placeholders or unlocated finds as hollow points. A placeholder marks a region in which the find was reported, not a find spot, so clusters of hollow points can share one coordinate. No accuracy class is inferred from the number of decimal places, and no coordinate has been moved. Records without coordinates remain in the list and table and can be isolated with the Map position filter.

Source wording and display fields

Original cell values are retained for every source column. Spaces and underscores are normalized for readable names in the interface; the embedded source values retain the original spelling. Specimen-age text is reproduced in the wording and notation recorded from the sources, without translation or standardization. Blank cells, not_available, not_applicable and ? remain distinct source values. Material tags are derived from the current Parts_found descriptions; phrases that describe missing parts (“no head”, “missing trunk and tail”) are ignored when tagging, and “Whole / near-whole” follows the workbook’s Complete? field. Tags are browsing aids, not a replacement for the description or a claim that a missing tag means a material was absent. Text found in header-less workbook columns is shown in the record as a workbook note.

Individual localities and map positions

Each specimen remains an individual point. When symbols overlap, the interface separates them on screen and draws fine leader lines or globe stems back to their coordinate anchors. This is a legibility adjustment, not a new locality, a change to coordinates, or a measure of spatial uncertainty. The underlying latitude and longitude do not change.

The flat map uses a rectangular 28–86°N frame with separate horizontal and vertical scaling. Use it to locate records, not to measure area or distance. The 3D polar globe retains the coastline geometry, rotation, and raised points, with a palette coordinated with the Lab’s website. Only the facing surface is selectable. Coastline geometry comes from the original HTML; its source attribution was not supplied.

Source values to review

These notes flag possible issues while retaining the workbook values. They also appear in the affected records:

  • #9 (row 6): A coordinate cell is stored as text (with stray spacing) rather than a number. It was read numerically; check the cell format in the workbook.
  • #9 (row 6): Location provenance is 'unlocated' but the workbook supplies coordinates. The point is drawn as a placeholder position only.
  • #23 (row 17): The collection year precedes the discovery year in the workbook.
  • #148 (row 18): No coordinates are recorded for this find; the record appears in the list and table but not on the map.
  • #34 (row 23): Age_BP_radiocarbon reads not_applicable while every other undated record uses not_available. Both are treated as 'no numeric age'.
  • #84 (row 44): Blue Babe (Pearl Creek, near Fairbanks, Alaska) has longitude -47.3166666667 in this workbook; the previous edition and the locality imply about -147.32. As entered, the point plots in Greenland. The value has not been changed; please check the cell.
  • #92 (row 48): The find coordinate (59.94, 30.31) lies at the St. Petersburg collection city while Location_found says Yuribei_River and the provenance label is regional_placeholder. Check whether find and collection coordinates were interchanged. The source value is retained.
  • #149 (row 56): The age text “31.700 ± 1.300” uses a period between digit groups. The map reads it as a thousands separator (31.700 = 31,700). Confirm the notation in the source.
  • #143 (row 81): The workbook longitude is positive (+138.825), so Nun Cho Ga (Eureka Creek, Yukon) plots at 138.825°E in Siberia rather than 138.825°W. No hemisphere correction has been applied; check the sign.
  • #146 (row 84): The age text “>50 0000 years” has ambiguous digit grouping. It is shown verbatim and excluded from numeric age bands until clarified.
  • #146 (row 84): An unlabelled workbook note says the corpse is old enough to defy standard radiocarbon dating; the age text is retained verbatim.

Discovery years and specimen ages

Discovery sites uses recorded find years, spanning 1692–2024 in this workbook. Collection year is used as a fallback where needed, and the raw date fields remain visible. Discovery chronology filters and its play control apply to this view.

Specimen ages has its own age interval controls and an individual record plot, using the text in Age_BP_radiocarbon. It does not inherit the discovery-year filter. Each plotted row corresponds to one specimen. Ranges and stated ± uncertainty are retained as intervals, and arrows show lower limits. Where a record gives multiple ages, each is evaluated separately; the space between alternative values is not treated as an age range. Digit groups separated by a space, comma or period (“29 500”, “34, 450”, “31.700”) are read as thousands. Selecting an interval finds compatible source values, not a claim that every matching specimen has a known age within that interval.

Original age text is not translated, standardized or recalibrated. BP conventionally refers to years before 1950, but calibration and notation differ between sources. The numeric display does not make these timescales equivalent. Records without an interpretable numeric age remain visible under All recorded ages and are omitted only from an active age interval. Table lists the matching records without a map or time graphic. Active time filters are retained as removable filter labels above the table.

Using the atlas

Taxon and provenance choices are inclusive; material filters match any selected tag. Counts reflect the other active filters. Discovery bars select a decade. Every map point opens its individual record; the age plot also opens records. Ctrl/⌘ + scroll zooms the map; ordinary scrolling moves the page. All data, coastline geometry and the globe renderer are embedded in this HTML, so the page works offline and makes no network requests.
